# Ziv, Fast evaluation of elementary mathematical functions (1991)

## Why this source

Ziv's strategy is how a finite computation delivers correctly rounded
transcendental values without solving the table maker's dilemma per
input: evaluate with a bounded error at increasing working precision
until the enclosing interval rounds unambiguously. `src/math/ziv.rs`
implements the driver with an interval test, cancellation boosting, and
a calibrated per kernel guard schedule; pfloat-libm wraps a second
directed pair loop around it at the fixed format boundary (ADR-0057).

## What it grounds

The `ziv_round` driver and its calibration table, the retry escalation
the kernels share, and the libm shell's outer loop. The known
divergences from the naive form are recorded in tree: the interval test
replaces recompute and compare (false convergence on hard to round
cases), exact value inputs defeat the loop and need pre Ziv dispatch,
and cancellation to zero needs a kernel side short circuit.

## Alternatives

Exhaustive worst case precomputation (the Lefevre and Muller route used
by CRlibm) bounds the needed precision a priori but only exists for
binary64; at arbitrary precision the iterative strategy is the only
general one, which is also why MPFR uses it.
